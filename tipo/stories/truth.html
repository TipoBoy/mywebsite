The Real Story

At the time I and my family were living in Perth Australia and we were intending to emigrate to Ireland.

I had been to Ireland before and got the impression that there was a tendency for cars to be driven into the ground - at least outside Dublin and we were not going there.. Also, cars were generally cheaper in the UK, even after paying Irish Vehicle Registration Tax (VRT) - This is an Import Duty in all but name and the EU mandarins in Brussels should give the Irish government a lot of grief over it.

I have had previous experience buying a car in London. It takes time. If you don't have someone to drive you around to look at cars it is going to cost you a bit in either taxi fares or to hire a car. It will cost you even more if you allow several days for your search and have to stay in a hotel - a family remember.

So, I had thought that if I could use the Internet to at least get a list of cars to have a look at It would lessen out costs in terms of time spent looking.

So I used the Net to do research on prospective cars. I had initially thought of getting an Audi 80 but they seemed to be quite expensive for ones with low mileage. I happened upon some on-line reviews of the Tipo and they were all very positive. I was particularly attracted by the extensive anti-corrosion. The one thing the reviews did say was that the 1.4L versions were a bit underpowered and that the 1.6L was the one to get. Then I discovered the Tipo Sedicivalvole home page - No, THAT was the one to get!, or at least a 2.0L.

From Perth I was able to search LOOT, Autotrader and some dealer resources. I searched these weekly for months to get an Idea of availability and prices.

I noticed that 2.0L 16v tipos with low mileage were not very common. A dealer did have one for sale with about 22,000 miles on it for about 7.5K a few weeks before we were due to leave Perth but when I rang up I was told it had sold very quickly.

I had tried to arrange a deal with a dealer in Tunbridge Wells on a 2.0L with about 34,000 miles on it but before the RAC could show up to inspect it - a one week waiting period!, useless - the car had been sold.

Then two weeks before we were due to leave I spotted John McFarlanes ad in Autotrader - a 3 door N Reg 2.0L 16v Tipo, 17,000m, for £8200.

This was easily the lowest mileage 16v I had seen in months of looking and only a two weeks before we were due to leave.

The only trouble was it was near Edinburgh and we were arriving at Heathrow. Then after thinking about it I reasoned after after travelling half way around the world what does a wee bit extra matter. So I rang John McFarlane and found that it was still for sale. I asked him lots of questions like had it ever been in a an accident etc. I said I was very interested and would he agree to hold the car for two weeks if I agreed to purchase it. He agreed. I tried to talk him down in price but he would not budge. So I agreed to the slightly higher than I would have liked price.

Now you have to be pretty insane to agree to buy a car for that much money sight unseen right?

Well first of all I was able to ring a company in the UK that would do a search to establish if there had been any insurance accident claims on the car or if it was listed as stolen - no. But it was under finance!

So I rang John back and explained the slight snag. Ahhh, the innocence of youth. He at first didn't see why that should present a problem as he was going to pay off the residual on the finance once I had paid him and before he left on his trip to Australia. I believed him - really. This happened to us once before when we bought my wifes X19. The girl selling it was also raising money to go on an overseas trip and quite genuinly did not understand our concern when we discovered that it was still under finance - her father explained it to her. Likewise with John. It really had not occured to him that some people might not repay the finance company - leaving you in possession of their property and a large debt.

So I rang the finance company and determined the payout figure so I could draw a cheque to pay them and pay John the remainder.

Apart from accident damage and legal ownership there was just mechanical condition? Well I suppose it is possible to trash a car in just 17,000 miles and 20 months but I judged from talking to John that he was not the type. In my book 17,000 miles is just run-in so I really couldn't see that there would be much chance of an serious mechanical faults and if there were I just wouldn't hand over the money.

John very kindly picked us up from Edinburgh airport as arranged and took as to various hotels before we finally found one that had room and wasn't too pricey. He really is a great guy and was very good to us. The car was just as he had described it - no signs of any accident damage and nothing wrong with it mechanically. A lovely car.

One other interesting bit to this story is that John was told when he bought the car that it was the last 2.0L 16v sold in the UK.

As for the newspapers version! I remember clearly telling John where I was ringing from at the outset. I do not recall any arrangement to meet at the Forth Bridge. As for travelling 11,000 miles to buy a Fiat - not even for a coupe turbo. On second thoughts...

We were travelling the 11,000 miles anyway. Going up to Edinburgh was the only bit you could say was out of our way but the cost and inconvenience was very small in the scheme of things and the distance from Stoneyburn to Cairnryan - the nearest Ferry port - was less than London-Fishguard.

Sorry to spoil such a nice story.

What did I go to so much trouble to buy? I got a very clean white N reg 3 door 2.0i 16v Tipo with ABS and about 18,000 miles on the clock and possibly the last one sold in the UK, for £8200. Now I will admit that I would have been happier, value wise, with a price around the £7400 mark but under the circumstances was prepared to pay a premium as pre-arranging a sale saved money elsewhere.

Giles Stewart
