Article found in Daily Record and Sunday Mail Online

I found the following article on 11th November 1997 in a Scottish newspaper. As you can see, it's about a guy called Giles Stewart who lived in Austrialia. Giles brought a FIAT Tipo 16v off a guy called John McFarlane who lived in Scotland. Pretty amazing stuff. Even more amazing is that almost exactly a year later on 13th November 1998 I received an email from Giles Stewart himself. He had found my web site and read the article printed by the newspaper for the first time. Giles feels that the newspaper article distorts things slightly and has provided me with his detailed version of the whole story.

11,000 MILES TO BUY A FIAT

A BARGAIN-hunting driver travelled 11,000 miles to buy a FIAT. John McFarlane advertised his Fiat Tipo on the Internet. He was thrilled when he got a call from Perth and asked the buyer to meet him at the Forth Bridge.

But he was staggered when Giles Stewart said he was calling from Perth, AUSTRALIA. John, 28, of Stoneyburn, West Lothian, said: "I couldn't believe it - it's just an ordinary 16-valve Fiat. It's not the most common car in Britain, but it's hardly the rarest either. "I really thought Giles was calling from Perth, Scotland."

Giles explained: "I'm emigrating to Ireland and wanted a car to take with me. I've tried to find a decent motor there, but the Irish seem to trash them until they break down. "I like Fiats, and the price was right."

Giles got the Tipo, with 16,990 miles on the clock, for £8200. An expert at Auto Trader magazine said: "It's a bit rare to get buyers from Australia."
