Brazilian FIAT Tipo 16V Modifications

All FIAT Tipo 16v cars exported to Brazil are subjected to the following modifications:
  • The ignition system is remapped to work with the brazilian gasoline, which consists of 22% alcohol.

  • The engine's power is reduced to 137 BHP due to the bad gasoline.

  • The complete fuel path is changed to one that can withstand the corrosive alcohol content of the gasoline.

  • The tyres and wheels are changed from 185/55R 15" diameter to 195/60R 14" diameter.

  • The suspension behaviour is changed, so that it can resist the bad roads and streets.

  • The car is fitted with anti-dust fences.
Only the 3 door 16v Tipo's were sold in Brazil, however a few 5 door models were imported. These were fortunate enough to have the option of tilting sliding Recaro seats.

A traffic law exists in Brazil preventing owners changing the original characteristics of the vehicle. This was not a problem until recently as the fines imposed were really cheap and the police almost never told you to "fix" it. Recently this has been enforced more rigourously. The fines have become expensive and now include points. Drivers have a limit of 20 points a year. Many Brazilian owners take the risk however and lower the suspension.

The price of Tipos in Brazil has plummeted and suffers one of the worst depreciations of any car there. Three years ago a new 16V Tipo would have cost £20,226 but now it is worth only £7,852. Compare this to the price of a new Tipo in the United Kingdom three years ago at £14,345.

Many imported 1600cc Fiat Tipos caught fire whilst parked. Fiat made two recalls to solve the problem. The first time it did not work and the problem kept happening. The problem was found only after an association of people that had problems with their Tipos went to court. If cars with power steering were parked and left on full lock the power steering fluid leaked onto the engine and caught fire. Although no 16V Tipos were involved the whole Tipo range was tarnished. One year later dealers stopped importing the 16V model as the import tax soared from 27 to 70 percent. The 1600cc continued to be imported, and was even manufactured in Brazil, but not any more. By the time Fiat started production in Brazil the car had already gained a bad image among the people due to the high depreciation. The Brazillian public also argued that the suspension transmitted a lot of noise to the interior.

In the United Kingdom the after market accessories are few and far between. However, in Brazil they are non existant let alone trying to find a mechanic trained to work on the car. The original 16V exhaust system is very expensive costing some £674 and very hard to find. Therefore, if the exhaust system rusts it is usually replaced by the more readily available smaller diameter 8v version.

It is very difficult to find a fully equipped Tipo 16V that has a sunroof, ABS brakes and an air-bag. Usually they have some incomplete combination.
