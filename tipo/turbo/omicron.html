Dear Mr Lee,

Thanks for your recent letter.

A turbocharged Fiat Tipo Sedicivalvole sounds an interesting project, but modifying cars like this are not cheap projects. It would certainly be more sensible in many ways to simply change car to something like an Integrale, as the values of modified cars do not come anywhere near to the cost invested in their creation..

If you're really serious, then I would suggest an engine swap would be the easiest way to provide the benefits of forced induction. It is possible to buy engines in various stages of completeness in Italy (estimated cost £2000 - £3000) or to buy a car and use that as a donor vehicle. I would suggest that installation of the engine in standard form should be the first phase, then if you decided that the engine needed more performance, we could consider that as an Evolution (that really should be Evoluzione) version.

I think it would be most sensible to retain the fuel injection and ignition system - I understand the Tipo 16V uses the Weber lAW system, this used by some Thema and Delta engines. The Thema and Delta both produced over 200bhp in their final forms - the Thema 16v Turbo (phase III) produced 205bhp and the Integrale produced 215bhp with the Evolution 2 KAT.

From these I would suggest that the Integrale 16v Evolution (I) engine is possibly the easiest to install from a fuel injection standpoint. This engine can be tuned to 260bhp before engine strength becomes an important issue. The quantity of power and torque the existing gearbox and driveshafis can take are other unknown quantities.

Costs - it is very hard (almost impossible) to estimate. Our hourly rate is £30 + VAT and we record time in 15 minute intervals. The conversion in Israel took about 10 days but there is no mention of how early they started each morning and how late they worked or how many people were working on the car - the time in man-hours is important. Ten days labour for one person, assuming normal 9am-5.30 pm would cost £2400 plus VAT. There may well be more time doing research, and how much test driving was done in that 10 days? This doesn't include any parts necessary to complete the job.

Warranty - We cannot provide a warranty on any length on unknown quantities, so without a comprehensive rebuild we would not be able to provide a warranty on the condition of the engine, or the transmission. A "brand new" engine will have been sitting for several years, and may not have been stored in the optimal conditions, but if sourced from Fiat/Lancia themselves may come with an adequate warranty. Its a very complex issue, requiring further discussion. I'm sure that you can understand our position, and we understand yours.

Deposit - we would require a deposit up front in the form of a proportion of the estimated costs to do the conversion, and would wish to treat the purchase of any engine or donor car as a separate transaction to be finalised before work took place. Work would have to include test driving and analysis before we could release the car to you. We would require payment of the balance then.

Tractability - the turbocharged Thema and Delta engines are very tractable and would be more than suitable for road or track days. Further tuning over standard would make the car less satisfactory for road use and more suitable for track days. This is another reason to change to another engine in standard form, and live with that for a while before deciding to modifying further. What you consider acceptable or otherwise may be different to another persons feeling.

Power - the only way of finding out power figures is to use an engine dynamometer - this measures the torque at the flywheel, and converting torque to power at a specific RPM is a very simple calculation. This has to be done with the engine out, and necessary fuel supply and exhaust system in place. This is how the manufacturers measure power and these are done in laboratory conditions using a known temperature, air pressure, humidity as well as their own cooling system. Even the type of fuel used makes a difference.

I would suggest booking a rolling road session with the engine in standard form - this would give torque and power figures for power at the wheels. This could be compared against the graph from Lancia for power at flywheel - the difference is due to power losses through the transmission - typically 10-20% for 2wd cars and 20-40% for 4WD cars.

If you then decided that further modifications are required, then another rolling road session afterwards would show improvements and then can be analysed for future fine tuning.

Credentials - Omicron Engineering has a lot of knowledge to do with the fundamental principles of turbocharging and engine design that other companies do not fully understand. I suggest you come and visit us and discuss the project further.
