Nikos Korovilas

Nikos, from Greece, has fitted the 20V Turbo engine from the Fiat Coupe into his 1.4 Fiat Tipo. Here are some of the features/specifications of the engine just incase you are unfamiliar with it:
  • 5 Cylinder, 20 Valve, 2 litre engine
  • 220 bhp @ 5750 rpm, 229 lb/ft @ 2500 rpm
  • Viscodrive viscous coupling applied to differential unit.
  • Counter rotating balancer shaft to ensure extremely smooth, quiet operation.
  • Aluminium alloy pistons with press fitted graphite inserts to limit friction.
  • Hydraulic tappets.
  • Twin overhead camshafts.
  • Electrohydraulic variable valve timing.
  • Phased sequential multipoint electronic injection.
  • Hot film controlled air flow.
  • Single coils on each spark plug.
  • Selective knock control in each cylinder.
  • Electronic ignition with static advance combined with the injection using Bosch Motronic M 2.10.4
  • Garrett TB 28 turbo charger
English is not Nikos' first language so I'm extremely grateful to him for making the effort to write to me about his car. As such, some of the information below may change as the exact details of the conversion become clear.

Nikos bought the Coupe from Switzerland for 2,000,000 Drachma (about £3600). He had the front chopped off and the rear subframe removed and shipped to Greece.

One of Nikos' best friends is a mechanic and he did the work for him. It took him 2 months but he was in no hurry and was working on other cars at the same time. The engine fits using the standard mounting points. However the gap between the left end of the engine and the inner wheel arch is very tight. This is the same when it's fitted into the Fiat Coupe. Unfortunately this means that the engine will need to be removed for the cam belts to be replaced. This is why the 72,000 service for the Coupe is so expensive. The alternator and air conditioning belt can be changed without removing the engine.

Nikos uses the Tipo key to open the doors. However he's fitted the Coupe's ignition lock and therefore uses the Coupe key to start the car. Unfortunately the ignition lock no longer locks the steering wheel. I don't understand why exactly, something to do with the light adjustment. The ignition system uses the Coupe KEYCODE ECU which immobilises the fuel pump and ignition coils. The Tipo ignition lock was replaced with the Coupe one and wired up using the loom from the Coupe. Nikos has also fitted part of the dashboard from the Coupe including the analogue clock and the heater A/C controls.

The Coupe and the Tipo share the same floorpan and therefore have exactly the same wheelbase of 2540mm. The Coupe is a wider car which means that the front and rear tracking is different from the Tipo 16V. Front on Coupe is 1491mm compared to Tipo's 1446mm, and the rear on Coupe is 1468mm compared to 1421mm on Tipo. This means that after Nikos replaced his Tipo's subframes and suspension with the Coupe's his wheels stuck out further. Compared to the Tipo 16V his front wheels stuck out 22.5mm more each side and the rears 23.5mm. He had to modify the wheel arches at the rear which were real easy and the fronts which were difficult although not imposible. The rear arches needed to be rolled and pulled out slightly. Gentle taps on the inside with a hammer on a block of wood were enough. The fronts wings needed the same work except that the plastic wheel arch protectors were extended to cover the gap created. He thinks that the only wheels that you can fit are the factory ones or the Cromodora 917 model with 205/45R16 tyres. You will also need spacers from the FIAT 128 model which give you about 3.5cm.

Nikos fitted the ABS system from the Coupe. The Coupe does not use electronic traction control and instead uses a mechanical ViscoDrive coupler on the differential. Nikos says that because the two cars have the same floorpan the holes for the pipes and wires of the ABS and Air conditioning units were already in his Tipo. He says the 4 pot Brembo calipers fitted to the Coupe are fantastic and seem to be even better when fitted to the Tipo.

Nikos used the Coupe exhaust system up to and including the catalytic converter. From the catalytic converter back it is standard Tipo although he is going to replace it soon.

Nikos used to have a Uno Turbo 1.4 racing that produced 180 bhp. He says that the handling of the two cars is like night and day. The Uno was useless at going around corners. His modified Tipo turns better than the original Coupe. Some guys from a magazine tested his car against a Coupe and his Tipo had much better handling. When cornering the Coupe was losing it's tail in the corners. They tried to make the Tipo do that by lifting off the accelerator but the car would not move an inch from its corse. He is using the standard shock absorbers and springs off the coupe and says they are comfortable at low speed but are stiff enough when the car is pushed.

Nikos has also been in contact with a guy called Armano who works for a company in Italy called Bergamo. They sell used parts of Italian cars at very good prices.Armano only speaks Italian though. Nikos bought off him a full Recaro black leather interior with red stitching which was fitted by Fiat to Tipo Sedicivalvoles made for the Italian market.
