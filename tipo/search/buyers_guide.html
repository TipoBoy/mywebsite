What to look for when buying a Tipo 16V

Good Oil Pressure

The oil pressure guage in the Tipo 16V measures up to 6 bar using 12 tick marks, each one therefore representing 0.5 bar.

Remember on a cold engine the oil pressure is always high, so wait until the engine oil is warmed up, then check the pressure. You also get better oil pressure if you use good oil (for example Agip Racing). Does it smoke when started up? This indicates worn valve guides.

Once warmed up, at tick over the needle should be on the second mark (1.0 bar), when doing 4000 rpm the needle should be straight up (3 bar). Taking the car to 6000 - 7000 rpm the needle should move up towards 5 bar not remain a 3.0 bar. If it doesn't then engine could be on it's way out.

Belts

It's vital that the belts (cam belt and balancer shaft belt) have been changed regularly. Fiat say at 63,000 miles but never leave it that long. It's much better to change them every say 20-30,000 miles. Whilst they are being replaced it's probably best to get the power steering belt changed as well as the only additional cost will be the belt.

If you can afford it, replace the water pump as well. Strictly unnessesary until it fails but when it does you will be faced with a hefty bill as the garage will need to remove all the amove belts to get to it.

If you are unsure whether they have been changed get them seen to as soon as possible. Read this if you are in any doubt about whether it's worth it.

Cooling System

Check for signs of leakage around cooling system hose joints.

Gearbox

Check to see if the gearchange is smooth. Does it slot into gear easily or do you have to struggle. If the gearbox needs to be replaced you will find great difficulty in finding a second hand one. Who's to say how good the second hand one will be and how long will it last. You could be looking at £400 labour to rebuild the existing gearbox. This excludes parts and actually removing and refitting the gearbox. The gearbox will last longer if you don't bang it between gears and don't crunch the gears. Check under the car for signs of oil leaks from gearbox.

Clutch

Does the clutch pedal sit higher than the brake pedal? This may indicate a worn master clutch cylinder.

Steering

Does the car steer straight when you take your hands off of the wheel. Is the steering wheel itself straight? Listen out for knocks from the front on full lock, indicating worn CV joints.

Springs and Dampers

Does the car roll around corners or stay flat? Physically push the car down with your hand and it should be quite firm. If it bounces around, it may mean worn shocks.

Electronics

A major problem on pre 1993 cars, so check that everything is working especially multi-function steering column stalks: find out what they do before test drive. Window lifts and electric sunroofs need careful checks. Check all the electrics/wipers/heater controls work.

Exhaust

Check the exhaust is in good condition and if the car has a catalytic converter that it's not been damaged.

Bodywork

Check the alignment of the panels. The actual fit of the panels is quite bad on the Tipo with huge gaps between the panels. However they should be even. No serious problems thanks to high percentage of galvanised protected steel. Check that inspection record has been kept up to date and that any repairs have been done correctly. Plastic tailgate has no corrosion problems.

Mileage

Check whether the mileage is genuine. Compare the mileage against the wear on the interior. Don't let the mileage put you off, judge the car overall. most if these cars now have big miles due to their age but they're still going strong.

Abused

Has the car been abused. Check the rims of the alloy wheels. Are the tyres wearing evenly. Has the interior been looked after or is it scruffy.

Brakes

On non 16V Tipos there is over-long pedal movement on pre-93 models. Much better feel on later cars. On the 2.0 16v the brakes can appear too sharp and over-servoed.

Paperwork

Run a HPI check. Costs about £30 but tells you whether the car has any outstanding finance on it. Check the service history and log book. Make sure the VIN numbers tie up. Check licence plate etchings on glass tie up with that on number plate.

Interior

Pre-spring 1992 models suffer from creaks and groans around the facia. Look for loose plastic trim panels, worn and baggy upholstery, threadbare carpets.

Average Life of Components

The following list is taken from Fast Lane magazine May 1993 who did a used car guide on the Tipo 16v.

  • Engine (major rebuild) - 100,000 miles
  • Gearbox (major rebuild ) - 100,000 miles
  • Fuel System - 100,000 miles
  • Ignition System - 100,000 miles
  • Charging System - 70,000 miles
  • Clutch - 40,000 miles
  • Exhaust System - 40,000 miles
  • Tyres - 30,000 miles
  • Body Perforation - 100,000 miles
  • Interior Wear - 60,000 miles
